# Responsible AI Guardrails

These guardrails apply across Sue Turnquist’s educator-facing AI projects.

## Intended use

- Define the user, instructional problem, and permitted task before building.
- Keep recommendations within the tool’s documented scope.
- Use AI to support judgment, not to make high-stakes student or personnel decisions.

## Privacy

- Do not submit student names, IDs, contact information, medical information, accommodation records, discipline records, or family information.
- Use anonymous examples, fictionalized cases, or aggregated data.
- Follow district policy and approved-tool requirements.

## Grounding and accuracy

- Require the system to use supplied criteria and knowledge sources.
- Ask for direct evidence and distinguish evidence from inference.
- State when information is missing or cannot be verified.
- Do not invent standards, lesson features, sources, quotations, results, or student needs.

## Bias, equity, and accessibility

- Check assumptions about culture, language, disability, income, technology access, and prior opportunity.
- Avoid deficit-based descriptions of learners.
- Evaluate whether recommendations create unequal participation or access.
- Provide accessible alternatives and preserve educator choice.

## Human oversight

- The educator or review team makes the final decision.
- Users should be able to accept, revise, or reject recommendations.
- High-risk, disputed, or policy-related decisions require escalation.

## Transparency

- Identify that AI supported the workflow.
- Document the prompt, sources, version, known limitations, testing date, and changes.
- Preserve a failure log and add confirmed failures to future test sets.
